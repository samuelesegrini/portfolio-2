> I am focusing my path on iOS development. I arrived here after working across distributed systems, algorithms, hardware, and interfaces—projects that taught me to see software as a complete product rather than a collection of screens or isolated classes.

## From Java to iOS

The first project that changed how I saw software was [Galaxy Trucker](/en/projects/galaxy-trucker-java-project/), a Java client–server game built with three other people. I worked at the points where networking, state, model, and interface had to agree. That is where I learned the value of explicit contracts and of verifying the behaviour of the whole system, not only its individual parts.

I later explored different problems: route planning in C, a hardware queue in VHDL and, through [SpinGO](/en/projects/spingo-sustainable-micromobility/), the relationship between user research, prototyping, and frontend implementation. I do not see these experiences as a random collection of technologies. They helped me understand that I want to work where technical structure and the quality of the experience have to support each other.

That is why I am now deepening my knowledge of Swift, SwiftUI, and UIKit. iOS interests me because it brings architecture, platform constraints, accessibility, and interaction details into the same conversation. It is where my interest in dependable systems meets my interest in clear, carefully finished products.

## How I work

I start with the problem and try to separate what I know from what I am still assuming. I make constraints explicit, build something verifiable, and use tests and documentation to preserve important decisions.

Teamwork taught me that a good interface is also an agreement between people. When I revisit a project with AI tools, I keep the same responsibility: an agent may accelerate exploration and implementation, but requirements, evaluation, and the result remain mine.

## What I am looking for

I am looking for my first opportunity in iOS development: a place where I can contribute to a real product, take part in code review and technical decisions, and keep growing alongside more experienced engineers. I bring broad technical curiosity, attention to detail, and a habit of describing both the results and the limits of my work honestly.
